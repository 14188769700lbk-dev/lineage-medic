## New-build brief — ClauseTrace

ClauseTrace is the best current fit for the DevNetwork Nutrient DWS sponsor challenge. It must be a genuinely new repository and implementation created after the August 17 opening time.

The implementation-ready specification is in [`claustrace-build-brief.md`](claustrace-build-brief.md). Do not initialize its project repository or write implementation code before the official opening time.

- Input: a contract, API change notice, or data-processing addendum in PDF form.
- DWS role: extract clauses and tables with confidence, render uncertain passages for human review, and sign/archive the final evidence artifact.
- Agent role: map accepted obligations to schema-migration constraints such as retention, compatibility windows, field restrictions, and approval owners.
- Safety boundary: the agent produces a review packet; it cannot approve or execute a production migration.
- Demo claim: use only synthetic documents unless a real document is explicitly authorized and sanitized.
- Commercial follow-through: offer the same workflow as a paid pre-migration contract review, without presenting it as legal advice.

The sponsor challenge offers free DWS event access but requires a Nutrient account and meaningful live API/SDK/Viewer use. Account creation and acceptance of provider terms require the account owner's confirmation. The event's overall Top 5 must be physically onsite in Santa Clara for the final pitch, so the current plan targets only the remotely judged sponsor challenge unless travel is separately confirmed.

- AI Builders and DevNetwork require work created during their event windows. Existing repositories may inform problem selection, but cannot be relabeled as new entries.
- Full-time data-engineering listings that demand unverifiable years of experience are excluded from immediate outreach.
- Reddit and community-channel promotion is deferred until the relevant self-promotion rules are checked for the exact community.

## Channel economics

- Hackathons offer asymmetric upside but no predictable revenue; treat prize value as probability-weighted, not earned income.
- Upwork can produce faster paid discovery, but proposals may require paid Connects and a truthful identity/profile. No proposal should be submitted until the account owner reviews any financial charge and identity claims.
- Open-source community sharing is free and credibility-aligned, but should ask for technical feedback rather than disguise a sales pitch.
- [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) accepts substantial projects that people can try without signup barriers. LineageMedic's hosted replay qualifies in form, but posting and replying publicly require the owner's confirmation and an existing HN account.
- The DataHub community explicitly supports sharing implementations and lessons learned; its Slack join flow creates a new external relationship and accepts DataHub terms, so it is a later, confirmed action rather than an automatic signup.
